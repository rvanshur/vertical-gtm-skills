# Client Profile Template

> **This is the single document that powers every skill in the suite.** Fill it out once for your
> company, save it as **`profiles/client-profile.md`**, and every skill reads it on its next run.
> Update it once and every skill inherits the change. When you change companies or clients, swap
> this one file. The methodology stays the same. The profile is the fuel.

---

## How To Use This Template

1. **Copy This Template** to `profiles/client-profile.md` (keep the template itself untouched).
2. **Fill Out The Sections Below** with your company's actual data. None should run longer than a page.
3. **Write In Operator Voice, Not Marketing Voice.** A skill can't score an account against "innovative".
4. **Refine Over Time.** After running a few skills you'll spot gaps. Add the missing data, and every skill picks up the change on its next run.
5. **Write `[RECORD]` where you have no data yet, never a guess.** A field marked `[RECORD]` tells every skill the team has not recorded that fact, so the skill treats it as unknown and says so in its output instead of scoring against an invented value. Fill it in from a real deal, a real call or a dated source when one exists. The PlotIQ example carries `[RECORD]` in its deal-history sections on purpose, because an invented company has no won deals to draw from.

A completed example lives at [`profiles/examples/plotiq-example.md`](examples/plotiq-example.md): PlotIQ, the made-up software company for cemeteries and funeral homes that every worked example in the library uses.

**Short On Time?** Fill **ICP Definitions** and **Competitive Landscape** first. Between them they feed 13 of the 14 sales skills. The full map of which section feeds which skill is in [`docs/customization.md`](../docs/customization.md#which-profile-sections-feed-which-skills).

### Quality Matters Here

The output of every skill is only as good as this file. "Improves efficiency" as a value proposition produces generic output. "Found 212 unsold plots in 90 days" produces output your reps can actually use in a conversation. Numbers, not adjectives.

## Competitive Landscape

Named competitors and where they break down. This is the section teams flinch at, and it is the single biggest output-quality lever you have. You're not putting this on your website. You're putting it in your stack. Be specific.

Every competitor or incumbent is filed under one of seven types, which Competitive Strategy (11) and Competitive Displacement (12) use as their starting list. Rename the types for your market if you need to, but keep seven, one per row:

1. **Direct Software** (a product that does what yours does)
2. **A Single-Function Tool** (does one part of the job well)
3. **A Service Bureau** (an outside firm does the work for a fee)
4. **A Manual Process** (spreadsheets, paper, memory)
5. **No Program At All** (nobody owns the job today)
6. **Outside Professionals** (an accountant, a lawyer, a consultant)
7. **An Adjacent Tool** (software bought for a neighbouring job, stretched to cover this one)

## Qualification Zones

Zones are groups of account traits your team agrees predict a win. Account Qualification, Research
Outbound, Meeting Prep and Daily Prospecting score every account against them, so a verdict means
the same thing in every skill. Four or five zones is plenty. Mark the ones you will not sell without
as must-haves.

| Zone | Must-Have? | HIGH | MEDIUM | LOW |
|------|-----------|------|--------|-----|
| 1. ___ | Yes / No | [What you can observe when this zone is strong] | ___ | [What rules it out] |
| 2. ___ | ___ | ___ | ___ | ___ |
| 3. ___ | ___ | ___ | ___ | ___ |
| 4. ___ | ___ | ___ | ___ | ___ |

Write every cell as something a rep can observe (a job posting, a system in use, a recent event),
never a feeling. PlotIQ's five zones are a worked example.

### Verdict Rule

- **GREENLIGHT:** at least two zones HIGH, and no must-have zone at LOW
- **MANUAL REVIEW:** neither GREENLIGHT nor DISQUALIFY
- **DISQUALIFY:** any disqualifier above, or every must-have zone at LOW

MEDIUM never counts as HIGH. Change the rule if your team qualifies differently, and the skills
follow what is written here.

| ___ | ___ | ___ | ___ | ___ |
| ___ | ___ | ___ | ___ | ___ |
| ___ | ___ | ___ | ___ | ___ |

Status takes one of three values, and the sales skills treat them differently:

- **Verified:** the customer confirmed the number and agreed to be named. Only Verified rows go into a talk track, a battlecard or an email a prospect will read.
- **Benchmark:** a published figure with its source and date named. Used for context, never as "our customer got this".
- **Illustrative:** invented for a worked example. Never cited to a prospect.

## Quick Validation Checklist

Before you save, run these three tests, then the detail checks:

- [ ] **The new-hire test.** Could a day-one BDR pre-qualify an account using only this file?
- [ ] **The swap test.** Would a competitor's version produce visibly different output? If not, it's too generic.
- [ ] **The proof-point test.** Can every persona be matched to a quantified case?
- [ ] **Personas have real language.** Are the pain descriptions from actual call recordings, not your pitch deck?
- [ ] **Competitive data is current.** Last updated within 90 days?
- [ ] **No internal jargon.** Would a prospect understand how you describe their pain?

If you can check all six boxes, your skills will produce high-quality output from day one.
