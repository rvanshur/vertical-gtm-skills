# Customize: GTM Positioning

A paste-in prompt that adapts this skill to your positioning challenge.

---

## The prompt

```
You are helping me adapt GTM Positioning to my specific market and pricing challenge.

Read the attached SKILL.md (gtm-positioning). It guides market positioning through April Dunford's six-step framework, pricing research through Van Westendorp and Gabor-Granger methods, and messaging house creation.

Your job is NOT to rewrite the framework. It is to help me apply it to my actual competitive landscape and build a position that survives customer validation.

Ask me these, ONE AT A TIME, and wait for each answer:

1. What are the alternatives your target customer is actually using right now? (Not just your competitors, spreadsheets, manual processes, consultants, doing nothing, etc.)

2. What can you do that those alternatives cannot do? (Be specific. "better UX" is not specific. "Automated 50-state compliance with real-time deadline alerts" is.)

3. What would your target customer have to believe to choose you over the alternatives? (What is the shift in their thinking?)

4. Have you validated this positioning with actual customers? If yes, what did you learn? If no, when will you test it?

5. What is your pricing hypothesis, and how did you arrive at it? (Guessed, benchmarked against competitors, researched willingness-to-pay, other?)

Then produce:

A. Your April Dunford positioning statement, built through the six-step framework with specific evidence for each step

B. Three pricing hypotheses (low/mid/high) with rationale for each, including unit economics implications

C. Your messaging house: unique value proposition, differentiation points, elevator pitches, messaging pillars, audience-specific messaging

D. A positioning validation plan, how you will test this positioning with real customers before scaling

E. THE HONEST PART: which parts of your positioning feel weakest, and what evidence would make you confident

Start with question 1.
```

---

## What you should expect to happen

Most founders sail through questions 1-3 and hit a wall on question 4. That is the validation question. If you have not tested positioning with real customers, you are working on hypothesis, not strategy.

Question 5 stalls when the answer is "we guessed" or "competitors do it so we do it."  Both are red flags. Real pricing is researched.

---

## If you are stuck on any question

- **Question 1 stalls:** You have not mapped competitive alternatives. Stop here. Map every way your customer could solve this problem today, including doing nothing. That is your real competition.
- **Question 2 stalls:** You cannot articulate what makes you different. That is the real finding. It is not that you do not have a difference, it is that you have not found it yet. Work through April Dunford framework to uncover it.
- **Question 3 stalls:** Your positioning is not coherent. The shift you need the customer to make is unclear. Rewrite until you can say it in one sentence.
- **Question 4 stalls:** You have not talked to customers since developing this positioning. That is fine. That is the next step. But do not call it validated until you have.
- **Question 5 stalls:** You priced based on vibes or competitors. Time to research it. Van Westendorp is quick (one survey, four questions). Gabor-Granger is iterative (multiple rounds).
```

---

## When you are ready for launch

After positioning is validated, you know:
- Who your customer is (specific role, company size, vertical)
- What problem you are solving and why it matters
- What alternatives exist and what makes yours different
- What price is defensible and produces healthy unit economics
- What message resonates with your target customer

When you have that clarity, you are ready for `/gtm-launch`. Launch takes your positioning and turns it into a motion and channel strategy.

If you skip validation and launch anyway, your message will land on the wrong ears. That always shows.
